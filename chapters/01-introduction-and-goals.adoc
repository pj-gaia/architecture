= Introduction and Goals

This section describes the relevant requirements and the driving forces that architects and the development team must consider.
It also lists the key quality goals and the relevant stakeholders and their expectations.

== Requirements Overview

Project Gaia is a software lifecycle and compliance toolsuite composed of integrated OSS applications and custom integration services.
It provides a unified platform for engineering quality assurance and release compliance auditing.

.Core functional requirements
[cols="1,4", options="header"]
|===
| ID | Requirement

| FR-01
| Engineers can track and review Software Bills of Materials (SBOMs) for all versioned product releases.

| FR-02
| Engineers are notified of known vulnerabilities in third-party dependencies as part of their existing workflow.

| FR-03
| Product Managers can view the quality and compliance status of a release from a single consolidated interface.

| FR-04
| Compliance policies and risk assessments (e.g., TARAs) are version-controlled and auditable.

| FR-05
| The platform exposes a unified API surface allowing integrations to be added without rewriting existing connections.

| FR-06
| All data flows between integrated OSS tools are traceable and do not require manual synchronisation.
|===

== Quality Goals

The top quality goals for the architecture in Priority order.

[cols="1,2,4", options="header"]
|===
| Priority | Quality Goal | Motivation

| 1
| Traceability
| Every compliance artifact (SBOM, policy, vulnerability finding) must be linkable back to a specific product version and release. This is the core value proposition.

| 2
| Extensibility
| New OSS tools must be integrable without breaking existing integrations. The platform is designed as a PoC with an expectation of growth.

| 3
| Operability
| The platform must be deployable and maintainable by a small team on Kubernetes without requiring specialist expertise per tool.

| 4
| Security
| Credentials, tokens, and secrets must not be embedded in configuration. All inter-service communication should be authenticated.

| 5
| Developer Experience
| Engineers should experience the platform as a single coherent tool, not a collection of loosely connected dashboards.
|===

== Stakeholders

[cols="2,2,4", options="header"]
|===
| Role | Contact / Representation | Expectations

| Product Manager
| Release approval owners
| Can audit any release for quality gate status and compliance posture without access to individual engineering tools.

| Software Engineer
| Development teams
| Single source of truth for quality gates; automated notifications on dependency vulnerabilities and policy violations.

| Security / Compliance Officer
| Compliance function
| Auditable, version-controlled policies with traceable evidence linking requirements to release artefacts.

| Platform / DevOps Engineer
| Infrastructure team
| Kubernetes-native deployment with Helm; each component operable independently.

| Architecture Board
| Technical leadership
| Clear separation between OSS components and integration glue; low vendor lock-in; documented decision rationale.
|===
